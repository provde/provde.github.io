USE CASES

Different workflows.
The same need for context.

When information gets rewritten, the details still matter. Bring the source into the review before the new wording is shared or used again.

01 / KNOWLEDGE BASES

Keep the policy clear, even in a shorter answer.

Support guidance often passes from a source document to a short article, then into an assistant's answer. A missing requirement can change what that answer tells someone to do.

See the changed wording beside its source, so the team can review the requirement before the update becomes guidance.

Explore the example →
Example review

SOURCE DOCUMENT

“Refunds are available within 30 days with a receipt.”

SHORTENED ANSWER

“Refunds are available within 30 days.”
What needs attention

The receipt requirement is missing.

02 / SUMMARIES & REPORTS

Keep the limits alongside the finding.

A confident summary can sound broader than the work behind it. Conditions, exceptions, and uncertainty are easy to lose as findings move into a report.

Make those differences visible during review, while the supporting text is still close at hand.

See the product →
Example review

SOURCE NOTE

“The improvement was observed in a small trial.”

REPORT SUMMARY

“The approach improves performance.”
What needs attention

A limited observation has become a general claim.

03 / TEAM HANDOFFS

Keep the original meaning in the handoff.

Meeting notes become action items. Action items become updates. A suggestion can turn into a commitment without anyone intending to make that decision.

Review the new wording against the original message before it becomes the next team's starting point.
